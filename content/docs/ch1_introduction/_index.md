### WHAT IS EPIDEMIOLOGY?

流行病學 is the study of how 疾病 is distrib-
uted in 族群 and the factors that inluence
or determine this 分布. Why does a 疾病
develop  in  some  people  and  not  in  others? The
premise  underlying  流行病學  is  that  疾病,
illness, and ill 健康 are not randomly distributed
in  human  族群. Rather,  each  of  us  has
certain  characteristics  that  predispose  us  to,  or
protect  us  against,  a  variety  of  different  疾病. These  characteristics  may  be  primarily  genetic  in
origin or may be the result of 暴露 to certain
環境al hazards. Perhaps most often, we are
dealing with an 交互作用 of genetic and environ-
mental factors in the development of 疾病.

A broader deinition of 流行病學 than that
given  above  has  been  widely  accepted. It  deines
流行病學 as “the study of the 分布 and
決定因素  of  健康-related  states  or  events  in
speciied  族群  and  the  application  of  this
study to 對照 of 健康 problems.”1 What is note-
worthy about this deinition is that it includes both
a  description  of  the  content  of  the  discipline  and
the purpose or application for which epidemiologic
investigations are carried out.


### THE OBJECTIVES OF EPIDEMIOLOGY

What  are  the  speciic  objectives  of  流行病學? First,  to  identify  the  病因學  or  cause  of  a  疾病
and  the  relevant  risk  factors—that  is,  factors  that
increase  a  person’s  risk  for  a  疾病. We  want  to
know  how  the  疾病  is  transmitted  from  one
person  to  another  or  from  a  nonhuman  宿主
to  a  human  族群. Our  ultimate  aim  is  to
intervene to reduce 發病率 and 死亡率 from
the  疾病. We  want  to  develop  a  比nal  basis
for  預防  programs. If  we  can  identify  the

etiologic or causal factors for 疾病 and reduce or
eliminate 暴露 to those factors, we can develop
a  basis  for  預防  programs. In  addition,  we
can  develop  appropriate  vaccines  and  治療s,
which can prevent the 傳播 of the 疾病
to others.

Second, to determine the extent of 疾病 found
in the 社區. What is the burden of 疾病 in
the 社區? This question is critical for plan-
ning 健康 services and facilities, and for training
future 健康 care providers.

Third, to study the 自然史 and 預後
of 疾病. Clearly, certain 疾病 are more severe
than others; some may be rapidly lethal while others
may have longer du比ns of 存活. Still others
are not fatal. We want to deine the baseline natural
history  of  a  疾病  in  quantitative  terms  so  that
as  we  develop  new  modes  of  intervention,  either
through  治療s  or  through  new  ways  of  pre-
venting complications, we can compare the results
of using such new modalities with the baseline data
in order to determine whether our new approaches
have truly been effective.

Fourth,  to  evaluate  both  existing  and  newly
developed  preventive  and  therapeutic  measures
and  modes  of  健康  care  delivery. For  example,
does  篩檢  men  for  prostate  cancer  using  the
prostate-speciic  antigen  (PSA)  test  improve  sur-
vival in people found to have prostate cancer? Has
the growth of managed care and other new systems
of  健康  care  delivery  and  健康  care  insurance
had  an  impact  on  the  健康  outcomes  of  the
patients involved and on their quality of life? If so,
what  has  been  the  nature  of  this  impact  and  how
can it be measured?

Fifth, to provide the foundation for developing
public  政策  relating  to  環境al  problems,
genetic  issues,  and  other  conside比ns  regarding
疾病  預防  and  健康  promotion. For

example,  is  the  electromagnetic  radiation  that  is
emitted  by  electric  blankets,  heating  pads,  and
other  household  appliances  a  hazard  to  human

particulate  matter  a  cause  of  adverse  acute  or
chronic  健康  effects  in  human  族群? Is
radon in homes a signiicant risk to human beings? Which  occupations  are  associated  with  increased
risks  of  疾病  in  workers,  and  what  types  of

### EPIDEMIOLOGY AND PREVENTION

A major use of epidemiologic evidence is to identify
subgroups  in  the  族群  who  are  at  high
risk for 疾病. Why should we identify such high-
risk groups? First, if we can identify these 高風險
groups,  we  can  direct  preventive  efforts,  such  as
篩檢  programs  for  early  疾病  detection,  to

interventions that are developed for the 疾病.

Second,  if  we  can  identify  such  groups,  we
may  be  able  to  identify  the  speciic  factors  or

characteristics  that  put  them  at  high  risk  and
then  try  to  modify  those  factors. It  is  important
to  keep  in  mind  that  such  risk  factors  may  be
of  two  types. Characteristics  such  as  age,  sex,  and
race,  for  example,  are  not  modiiable,  although
they  may  permit  us  to  identify  高風險  groups. On the other hand, characteristics such as obesity,
diet,  and  other  lifestyle  factors  may  be  potentially
modiiable  and  may  thus  provide  an  opportunity

aimed  at  reducing  or  changing  speciic  暴露
